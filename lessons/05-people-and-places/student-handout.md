# Lesson 5 — Људи, љубимци и места · People, pets, and places

## Today I can ...

- describe a person or pet with two details
- say where someone is
- describe a place and connect ideas
- read a short familiar passage in Cyrillic with controlled support

## Cumulative conversation

Greet someone, introduce one real person, then offer something and request it with `Mogu li da dobijem ...?` Continue naturally with one follow-up question.

## A small grammar map — nominative and accusative

The **nominative** identifies who someone is or who is being described: `Ovo je Ana.` (*This is Ana.*) and `Ana je visoka.` (*Ana is tall.*).

The **accusative** marks the direct object after an action: `Vidim Anu.` (*I see Ana.*) and `Opisujem Anu.` (*I am describing Ana.*). What changed? For many feminine nouns, the final `-a` changes to `-u`: `Ana → Anu`, `sestra → sestru`, `voda → vodu`.

Try two true pairs: identify someone with `Ovo je ...`, then say whom you see or describe with `Vidim ...` or `Opisujem ...`. Familiar phrases such as `imam jednog brata` remain useful whole chunks; masculine forms will be explored later.

## Listen and read

**Amaya:** Ko je ovo?  
*Who is this?*

**Marko:** Ovo je moja baka. Ona je veoma vesela i duhovita.<br>
*This is my grandmother. She is very cheerful and funny.*

**Amaya:** A gde je ona?  
*And where is she?*

**Marko:** Ona je u Beogradu. Beograd je veliki i živ.  
*She is in Belgrade. Belgrade is big and lively.*

One true character contrast to reuse later: `Амаја учи / студира. · Amaya uči / studira.` — Amaya studies. `Марко ради. · Marko radi.` — Marko works.

## Pets · Љубимци

`љубимац · ljubimac` — pet · `пас · pas` — dog · `мачка · mačka` — cat

Use a real family or pet photo. The same descriptions can express a true reaction: `Пас је мали и миран. · Pas je mali i miran.`

Select four descriptions that fit people or places you genuinely want to discuss. The rest are recognition items.

## Pattern to notice

Descriptions often change with the word they describe: `Marko je visok`; `Ana je visoka`; `grad je velik`; `kuća je velika`. Use meaningful pairs before trying to state a rule. Connect details with `i`, `ali`, and `veoma`.

## Pronunciation and Cyrillic

Recycle the familiar sound contrasts `č/ć` and `dž/đ` in already-known words before meeting their Cyrillic symbols. These are the final five letters not explicitly introduced in Lessons 1–4:

`Ч = Č` · `Ћ = Ć` · `Џ = DŽ` · `Ђ = Đ` · `У = U`

Decode: `кућа` (`kuća`), `дечко` (`dečko`), `Ђорђе` (`Đorđe`), `џем` (`džem`). Say the familiar word first, then connect sound and letter. After this set, all 30 Serbian Cyrillic letters have been introduced; Lesson 6 consolidates them in connected reading.

## Controlled Cyrillic reading

Read each familiar sentence in Cyrillic before checking Latin:

> Она је у Београду.  
> *Ona je u Beogradu.*

> Град је велики и жив.  
> *Grad je veliki i živ.*

Read the whole phrase once, identify only the blocking letter, and try again.

## Reading — “Ala je lep ovaj svet”

Read in Latin first for meaning and rhythm:

> Ala je lep ovaj svet,<br>
> onde potok, ovde cvet;<br>
> tamo njiva, ovde sad,<br>
> eno sunce, evo hlad.<br>
> Tamo Dunav, zlata pun,<br>
> onde trava, ovde žbun.<br>
> Slavuj peva, ne znam gdi —<br>
> ovde srce, ovde ti!

### Poem word bank

| Poem language | Meaning / note |
|---|---|
| `ala je lep` | how beautiful; an expressive exclamation |
| `ovaj svet` | this world |
| `onde / ovde / tamo` | over there / here / there |
| `eno / evo` | there is / here is; words used while pointing something out |
| `potok / cvet / njiva` | stream / flower / field |
| `sunce / hlad` | sun / shade |
| `Dunav` | the Danube River |
| `zlata pun` | full of gold; poetic word order |
| `trava / žbun` | grass / bush |
| `slavuj peva` | the nightingale sings |
| `ne znam` | I do not know |
| `gdi` | an older poetic form of `gde`; recognize it, but use `gde` in conversation |
| `srce / ti` | heart / you |

Then read the original in Cyrillic:

> Ала је леп овај свет,<br>
> онде поток, овде цвет;<br>
> тамо њива, овде сад,<br>
> ено сунце, ево хлад.<br>
> Тамо Дунав, злата пун,<br>
> онде трава, овде жбун.<br>
> Славуј пева, не знам гди —<br>
> овде срце, овде ти!

Jovan Jovanović Zmaj · public-domain text; source: Serbian Wikisource.

### Talk about it

1. Which words point to “here” and “there”?
2. What can the speaker see and hear?
3. What mood does the poem create?
4. Which two details would you change to describe the world around you today?
5. What person, pet, and place would you connect in your own short description?

Retell the passage, then connect it to your own world and ask a follow-up question.

## From the poem to your world

What would your version of `ovaj svet` include? Choose one real photo showing a meaningful person or pet in a place that matters. Give at least four connected sentences: who is there, where they are, two descriptions, and why they belong in your world. Use `i`, `ali`, or `veoma`. Your partner responds and asks a follow-up question; then switch roles.

## Optional repair prompts

Use only if conversation reveals a gap:

- Ana je visok / visoka.
- Grad je velik, ___ lep.
- ___ je Marko? — U Beogradu.

Answers: `visoka`; `ali` or `i`; `Gde`.
