# Evolving curriculum

The first six lessons form the starter course. Lessons are developed in small batches, and the sequence changes when session notes or learner requests show a stronger real-life need. Lessons 1–11 have been generated; Lessons 12 onward below are a proposed plan only.

| Lesson | Communication outcome | Main language pattern | Pronunciation / script |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Greet someone and introduce herself | `ja sam`, `ti si`; simple questions | Five vowels; `j`, `r`; recognize `А Е О Ј М` |
| 2 | Ask and answer how someone is | question intonation; `ti` and `Vi` | `č/ć`, `dž/đ`; recognize `К С И Т Х В Л` |
| 3 | Talk about close family | gender; `moj/moja`; `imam` | `lj`, `nj`; guided decoding with `Б Р Н П Д Ц Љ Њ` |

| 5 | Describe a person and a meaningful place | useful adjective pairs; `on/ona je`, `gde`, connectors | introduce final letters `Ч Ћ Џ Ђ У`; first controlled independent Cyrillic reading |

| 7 | Discuss preferences across family, music, food, and pets | `volim`, `obožavam`, `više volim ... nego`, `zato što` | hard `č/dž` vs. soft `ć/đ` discrimination; Cyrillic-led bridge; Latin supports new language |

## Adaptations from session notes

After Lesson 6, the learner asked for more scenarios and question-asking, a little more vocabulary, grammar only in small doses, and Latin for long words in Cyrillic readings. Lessons 7–11 therefore include:

- two or three short role-play scenarios with teacher “push moves” that require further questions
- 7–30 new items per lesson, with closed sets such as numbers, days, and colors at the upper end
- grammar shown as one-slide patterns attached to useful chunks
- a Latin-help footer under every Cyrillic reading slide, and a matching line in the handout
- a hard `ч/џ` vs. soft `ћ/ђ` listening and production exercise in Lesson 7, maintained briefly through Lesson 11

## Proposed ordering rationale

1. **Numbers before calendar:** dates, years, times, and holiday planning all depend on number confidence.
2. **Calendar before colors:** calendar language immediately supports making plans; colors then provide a lighter descriptive lesson while recycling adjective agreement from Lesson 5.
3. **Colors before school:** the school lesson can naturally reuse numbers and colors with books, pens, classroom objects, schedules, and subjects.
4. **Deferred outcomes afterward:** location language, expanded past narration, the family meal, and cumulative conversation remain in the plan rather than being displaced.

Do not generate Lessons 12–16 until requested. Reassess the ordering after each new session note; in particular, the school theme may move earlier if it becomes immediately relevant.

## Script progression

Cyrillic is introduced through Serbian the learner already understands: hear and understand first, read in Latin, notice a small letter set, and then decode the familiar language in Cyrillic. Lessons 1–2 emphasize recognition; Lessons 3–6 develop supported and then independent decoding; Lesson 7 is the Cyrillic-led bridge; Lesson 8 onward is primarily Cyrillic while Latin remains an actively practiced real-world Serbian script. The pacing should follow demonstrated reading comfort rather than the lesson number alone.

## Completion evidence

A lesson is complete when the learner can perform its communication outcome with light prompting. Finishing a worksheet alone is not completion.

Use dated files in `notes/` rather than a separate progress log. After Lessons 6, 12, and 16, record a short conversation and review:

- What she can do without notes
- Which phrases require prompts
- Which sounds reduce intelligibility
- Which topics motivate her
- What should be recycled, slowed down, or accelerated
