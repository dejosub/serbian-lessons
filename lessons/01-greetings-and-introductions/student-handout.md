# Lesson 1 — Greetings and introductions

## Today I can ...

- greet someone
- say my name
- ask another person's name
- say that I am learning Serbian

## Listen and read

**Ana:** Zdravo! Ja sam Ana. Kako se zoveš?  
*Hi! I’m Ana. What’s your name?*

**Emi:** Zdravo! Ja sam Emi. Drago mi je.  
*Hi! I’m Emmy. Nice to meet you.*

**Ana:** Drago mi je. Učiš srpski?  
*Nice to meet you. Are you learning Serbian?*

**Emi:** Da, učim srpski. Malo!  
*Yes, I’m learning Serbian. A little!*

## Useful language

| Serbian (Latin) | Serbian (Cyrillic) | English | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Zdravo! | Здраво! | Hi! / Hello! | friendly, common |
| Dobar dan! | Добар дан! | Good afternoon! / Hello! | polite, daytime |
| Ja sam ... | Ја сам ... | I am ... | introduce yourself |
| Kako se zoveš? | Како се зовеш? | What is your name? | informal, one person |
| Kako se zovete? | Како се зовете? | What is your name? | formal or plural |
| Drago mi je. | Драго ми је. | Nice to meet you. | literally “glad to me is” |
| Da. | Да. | Yes. | |
| Ne. | Не. | No. | |
| Učim srpski. | Учим српски. | I’m learning Serbian. | |
| Malo. | Мало. | A little. | |
| Hvala. | Хвала. | Thank you. | |
| Vidimo se! | Видимо се! | See you! | friendly goodbye |

## Pronunciation: say what you see

Serbian has five steady vowel sounds:

- `a` like **a** in *father*
- `e` like **e** in *met*
- `i` like **ee** in *see*
- `o` like **o** in *off*, kept short and pure
- `u` like **oo** in *food*

`j` sounds like English **y** in *yes*: `ja`, `je`. Serbian `r` is tapped or rolled; begin with a light tap and do not delay speaking while perfecting it.

Read aloud: `Ana — Emi — dobro — učim — ja — je — Drago mi je.`

## Pattern: ja sam / ti si

| Person | Serbian | English |
|---|---|---|
| ja | ja sam | I am |
| ti | ti si | you are (informal singular) |

Serbian often leaves out `ja` or `ti` when the meaning is clear: `Ja sam Emi` and `Emi sam` are not interchangeable; for introductions, use `Ja sam Emi` or simply `Emi` after the question.
