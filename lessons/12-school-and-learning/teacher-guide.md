# Lesson 12 teacher guide — School and learning

## Target

Amaya can name things used for learning, talk about school subjects, teachers, homework, and grades, and say what she and someone else read, write, and learn with the correct `-ам / -им / -ем` ending. In a two-minute exchange she can ask for help, ask what a word means and how to say something, and slow a fast speaker down.

## Before the lesson

- Read the Lesson 11 session note. If Lesson 11 ended early, its unfinished reading or shop scenario can replace the warm-up here; keep it self-contained and don’t rely on what she read at home.
- Put real objects on the table: a book, two notebooks of different colors, a pencil, a ballpoint pen, and, if you have one, a small whiteboard.
- Think of three Serbian words a nine-year-old might not know in English and three English words for Luka to ask about (for example `notebook`, `teacher`, `easy`).
- Recall your own or Marko’s school in Serbia: start time, a favorite subject, a strict or kind teacher. Real details make the reading discussion come alive.
- For the word-origins slide, let her guess first; she is a linguist and enjoys working it out.

## Sequence — 60 minutes

1. **Warm-up: What am I thinking of? — 6 min:** You think of something first and answer only `Да` or `Не`; let silence push her to the next question. Then switch. Colors and `Да ли је ...?` are the targets.
2. **At school: things — 4 min:** Touch each object: `Шта је ово? Које је боје?` She answers, then asks you.
3. **School and subjects — 5 min:** Go through the words, then `Који ти је омиљени предмет?` for both of you. Mention that a secondary-school teacher is a `професор`.
4. **Word origins — 5 min:** Write `уч-` and ask her to find it in `учитељ`, `ученик`, `учионица`, `учење`; she guesses what each ending adds. Then show `оловка`, `свеска`, `предмет`, `школа` and let her guess each story before you reveal it. Link back to the day names from Lesson 10 (`понедељак` = after `недеља`).
5. **Grammar slide: the present tense — 12 min:** Start from the role: the ending tells you who does it, so `ја` disappears. Point at `Учим српски.` Then name the three groups from forms she already uses (`имам`, `волим`, `разумем`). Work through the example bank one verb at a time: she says a true sentence about herself (`Читам књигу.`), asks you (`Шта читаш?`), then says one about Marko (`Марко чита новине.`). Mention `писати → пишем` once. Add the row to the grammar map.
6. **Ask for help — 4 min:** Model each question with natural rhythm and let her copy the melody. Then she uses each one on you for real: one word she wants to know, one she doesn’t understand.
7. **Dialogue — 4 min:** Listen with print hidden: what does Luka need, and what is his grade? Then switch roles.
8. **Reading — 7 min:** Read once for the school day, once for what was easy and hard. The Latin help covers the long words; past-tense forms like `ишао`, `волео`, `играо` are for reading, not production.
9. **Discussion — 5 min:** Start with question 3 about her own school; move to grades and scripts.
10. **Scenarios — 6 min:** Help Luka with his homework (you play Luka); First day at a Serbian course if time remains, otherwise as homework with Marko.
11. **Exit — 2 min**

## Grammar example bank — present tense

Work through these one at a time; for each, a true sentence about herself, then a question to you or a sentence about Marko.

- `-ам`: `читам`, `имам`, `знам`, `гледам`, `слушам`, `свирам` (`Свирам бас.`), `шетам` (`Шетам пса.`)
- `-им`: `учим`, `говорим`, `радим`, `волим`, `носим`, `видим`
- `-ем`: `пишем`, `разумем`, `једем`, `идем`, `пијем` (`Пијем кафу.`)
- irregular, just as they are: `могу / можеш / може`, `хоћу / хоћеш / хоће`, `сам / си / је`

Sorting tip: the `ја` form tells the group; the `он/она` form is the `ја` form without its last letter (`читам → чита`, `учим → учи`, `пишем → пише`).

## Push moves

- As Luka, speak a little too fast once and wait for `Не разумем. Полако, молим.`
- In the warm-up, answer a question with `Па, мало ...` (“well, a bit”) and see whether she asks again.
- As the professor, ask `Шта читате на српском?` and `Да ли пишете ћирилицу?`—she needs the `-ам / -ем` forms to answer.
- In discussion, ask `А шта Марко учи?` to get an `он` form.

## Boundaries

The present-tense singular is the grammar focus: correct the ending only in `ја`, `ти`, and `он/она` forms she produces. Plural verb forms (`читамо`, `учите`) are recognition only. `из енглеског`, `о својој школи`, and `у првом разреду` are chunks in the dialogue and reading; don’t explain those cases now. Past forms in the reading are for comprehension.

## Script and pronunciation

Primarily Cyrillic; every new word and question on the slides has its Latin beside it, and long words in the reading are in Latin at the bottom. The pronunciation focus is natural question rhythm for classroom questions: one smooth phrase, with the voice rising on the important word (`Шта значи ...?`, `Можеш ли да ми помогнеш?`). Use the cultural note to point out that Serbian children learn Cyrillic first.
