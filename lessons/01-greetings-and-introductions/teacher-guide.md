# Lesson 1 teacher guide — Greetings and introductions

## Target

By the end of 45–60 minutes, the learner can greet you, exchange names, and say `Učim srpski` in a short unscripted exchange.

## Before the lesson

- Complete `course-profile.md`, especially the dialect choice.
- Print the student handout and homework.
- Record the dialogue twice: once naturally and once slowly, with clear phrasing but no exaggerated English-style stress.
- Choose the real name used in the role-play.

## Suggested sequence

### 1. Warm start — 5 minutes

Say `Zdravo` and introduce yourself. Let her imitate before explaining. Ask what Serbian she already recognizes and record the baseline afterward.

### 2. Dialogue — 10 minutes

Play or perform the dialogue once with the page hidden. Ask only what she recognized. Then listen while following the text. Perform it together, switching roles.

### 3. New language — 10 minutes

Practice the 12 items in short exchanges. Treat `Kako se zovete?` as recognition-only unless she expects formal situations soon.

### 4. Pronunciation — 8 minutes

Model the five vowels and `j`. Use listen → pause → repeat. Accept an approximate `r`; intelligible, confident speech matters more in Lesson 1.

### 5. Pattern and exercises — 10 minutes

Contrast `ja sam` and `ti si` with names and identities. Do not introduce the full present tense of *biti*. Complete Practices A and B orally before writing.

### 6. Personal role-play — 10 minutes

Meet at an imaginary family gathering. First allow the handout, then remove it. Add one friendly family member and repeat. Correct only the target phrases and the five vowel sounds.

### 7. Exit check — 5 minutes

Without notes, prompt a fresh greeting. Mark independently produced phrases in `progress.md`. Assign the small homework.

## Answer key

Practice A: 1–c, 2–d, 3–e, 4–a, 5–b.

Practice B: `Zdravo! Ja sam Mina. Kako se zoveš? — Ja sam Sara. Drago mi je.`

Tiny Cyrillic preview: `Ja sam` (“I am”).

## What to observe

- Does she distinguish each vowel without turning it into a diphthong?
- Does `j` sound like English *y* rather than English *j*?
- Can she retrieve the name question without reading?
- Is the amount of Cyrillic interesting, neutral, or distracting?
- Which real-life person should appear in Lesson 2?
