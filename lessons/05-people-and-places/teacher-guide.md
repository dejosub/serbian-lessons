# Lesson 5 teacher guide — People and places

## Target

Without notes, Amaya can describe one real family member or pet and one meaningful place in at least four connected Serbian sentences and respond to a follow-up question.

## Before the lesson

- Ask for two digital photos: a person and a place Amaya wants to discuss.
- Prepare a natural and slow recording of the dialogue and reading.
- Select four descriptions that are personally useful; leave the rest as recognition vocabulary.
- Read the latest session note and choose one table phrase and one pronunciation contrast to recycle.

## Sequence — 60 minutes

1. **Cumulative conversation — 6 min:** Greeting, person introduction, and a short table exchange that retrieves `Mogu li da dobijem ...?`. Follow Amaya’s meaning while noting one repair target.
2. **Photo hook — 5 min:** Show a meaningful person or place and give two comprehensible descriptions. Invite guesses and reactions.
3. **Dialogue listening and performance — 7 min:** Listen for person, place, and descriptions; read in Latin; switch roles and personalize. Establish the true contrast `Амаја учи / студира; Марко ради` for recycling in later lessons.
4. **High-value descriptions and case map — 7 min:** Select and use four relevant adjectives in true statements. Contrast `Ovo je Ana / Ana je visoka` with `Vidim Anu / Opisujem Anu`; ask what changed before naming nominative and accusative. Highlight only the common feminine `-a → -u` pattern, personalize two pairs, and return to conversation. Treat `imam jednog brata` as a familiar chunk rather than explaining masculine animacy or numeral agreement here.
5. **Poem in Latin — 7 min:** Read “Ala je lep ovaj svet” for scene, rhythm, and repeated location words. Use the short key-word line on each poem slide for immediate support and the fuller handout table only when needed. Briefly explain expressive `ala`, pointing words `eno/evo`, poetic `zlata pun`, and older `gdi`; do not test every noun.
6. **Discussion and Cyrillic rereading — 8 min:** React to the poem, personalize two images, then reread it in Cyrillic. Explain `gdi` as poetic recognition-only language.
7. **Connected speech — 5 min:** Expand isolated descriptions using `i`, `ali`, and `veoma`; ask natural follow-up questions.
8. **Pronunciation and Cyrillic — 7 min:** Recycle the already-practiced sounds `č/ć`, `dž/đ`, and pure `e/o`; explicitly distinguish familiar sounds from the new symbols `Ч Ћ Џ Ђ`, add `У`, and note that these are the final five letters not introduced in Lessons 1–4. Perform controlled phrase reading; Lesson 6 will consolidate the complete alphabet.
9. **From the poem to your world — 6 min:** Ask what Amaya’s version of `ovaj svet` would include. She chooses one meaningful person or pet in one place, gives four connected sentences, explains why the subject belongs in her world, and answers a partner follow-up; then reverse roles.
10. **Exit performance — 2 min:** Describe a new person-or-pet-in-a-place pairing without notes.

## Correction focus

Prioritize intelligible target sounds and one usable adjective form at a time. Recast an error, ask for one immediate reuse, and return to the conversation.

## Cyrillic boundary

This is controlled independent reading of familiar language. Meaning and pronunciation still come first; Latin remains available after an initial Cyrillic attempt. Do not turn the reading into a speed test.

## Session note for Lesson 6

Add a dated file in `notes/` recording independently produced connected sentences, useful versus unused descriptions, one recurring form or pronunciation issue, Cyrillic reading behavior, and the topics that sustained open discussion.
