# Lesson 2 — How are you?

## Today I can ...

- ask how someone is
- answer naturally in several ways
- choose informal `ti` or polite `Vi`
- hear and practice `č/ć` and `dž/đ`

## Review

Without looking, say: hello; your name; “What’s your name?”; “Nice to meet you”; “I’m learning Serbian.”

## Listen and read

**Mila:** Zdravo, Emi! Kako si?  
**Emi:** Dobro sam, hvala. A ti?  
**Mila:** Odlično sam!  

**Emi:** Dobar dan, gospođo Petrović. Kako ste?  
**Gospođa Petrović:** Dobro sam, hvala. A Vi?  
**Emi:** I ja sam dobro.

## Useful language

| Latin | English | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Kako si? | How are you? | informal, one person |
| Kako ste? | How are you? | polite or plural |
| Dobro sam. | I’m well. | neutral |
| Odlično sam. | I’m great. | positive |
| Nije loše. | Not bad. | common, neutral |
| Umorna sam. | I’m tired. | female speaker |
| Umoran sam. | I’m tired. | male speaker |
| Hvala. | Thank you. | |
| A ti? | And you? | informal |
| A Vi? | And you? | polite; capital often used |
| I ja. | Me too. | literally “and I” |
| Vidimo se. | See you. | |

## `ti` or `Vi`?

Use `ti` with one friend, close relative, child, or someone who invites it. Use `Vi` with an unfamiliar adult, in formal situations, or to show respect. `Vi` also means “you all”; context tells you which meaning applies.

## Pronunciation: `č/ć` and `dž/đ`

- `č` is a firmer **ch**: `čaj` (tea), `odlično` (great).
- `ć` is softer, with the tongue farther forward: `ćao` (hi/bye), `kuća` (house).
- `dž` is a firmer **j**: `džem` (jam).
- `đ` is softer: `gospođa` (Mrs./madam), `Đorđe` (George).

Do not let these sounds stop the conversation. Listen, imitate, and improve gradually.

The Cyrillic spellings `ч/ћ` and `џ/ђ` are a preview today, not decoding targets. First learn to hear and say the contrasts in Latin script.

## Cyrillic bridge

The language is already familiar. Add these letters to Lesson 1’s set:

`К = K` · `С = S` · `И = I` · `Т = T` · `Х = H` · `В = V` · `Л = L`

Cyrillic `С` sounds like Latin `S`, and Cyrillic `В` sounds like Latin `V`.

Decode with support:

- `Како си?` — `Kako si?`
- `А ти?` — `A ti?`
- `Хвала.` — `Hvala.`

Read the Cyrillic first, then check the Latin. Do not try to decode the other lesson sentences in Cyrillic yet.

## Pattern

Question → answer → return the question:

> Kako si? — Dobro sam, hvala. A ti?  
> Kako ste? — Dobro sam, hvala. A Vi?

## Practice A — Choose `si` or `ste`

1. To a close friend: Kako ___?  
2. To an unfamiliar older adult: Kako ___?  
3. To two cousins together: Kako ___?  
4. To your partner: Kako ___?

## Practice B — Complete

Use: `dobro`, `hvala`, `ti`, `ste`, `odlično`.

1. Kako si? — ______ sam, ______. A ______?  
2. Kako ______? — ______ sam!

## Practice C — Make it real

Ask three times. Answer differently each time: `Dobro sam`, `Odlično sam`, `Nije loše`, or `Umorna/Umoran sam`. Use `ti` once and `Vi` once.

## Reading — A quick call

Mila zove Emi. Emi je kod kuće. „Zdravo! Kako si?” pita Mila. „Umorna sam, ali dobro sam,” kaže Emi. Mila kaže: „I ja sam umorna!”

*Mila calls Emi. Emi is at home. “Hi! How are you?” Mila asks. “I’m tired, but I’m well,” Emi says. Mila says, “I’m tired too!”*

New language: `zove` — calls; `kod kuće` — at home; `pita` — asks; `ali` — but; `kaže` — says.

### Talk about it

1. Who calls whom?
2. How does Emi feel? How does Mila feel?
3. Why might they be tired? Invent a reason.
4. What would your true answer be today?

Use Serbian where you can and English where you need it. Then retell the call in your own words.

## Answer key

Practice A: 1 `si`, 2 `ste`, 3 `ste`, 4 `si`.

Practice B: 1 `Dobro`, `hvala`, `ti`; 2 `ste`, `Odlično`.
