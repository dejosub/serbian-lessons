# Lesson 12 homework — Домаћи задатак

Choose three activities, about ten minutes each.

1. **Read and retell:** Read “Марко у школи” in Cyrillic, using the Latin help. Retell Marko’s school day without looking: when school started, what was in his bag, what was easy and hard, and what he did after school.
2. **My school days voice note:** Record 60–90 seconds about your own school: what your favorite subject was, what was easy or hard, and what grades looked like. Then say what you learn, read, and write now: `Сада учим ...`, `Читам ...`, `Пишем ...`
3. **Word detective:** Find three more Serbian words built from a root you already know—for example from `читати`, `писати`, `радити`, or `продавати`. For each, guess what the ending adds, then check with Marko or a dictionary.
4. **Conversation with Marko:** Ask Marko about his school in Serbia: `Који ти је био омиљени предмет?`, `Шта ти је било тешко?`, `Ко ти је била учитељица?` Use `Шта значи ...?` and `Можеш ли да поновиш?` at least once each.
5. **First day at a Serbian course with Marko (Ви):** Marko is the professor. Introduce yourself, say what you read and write in Serbian, ask how to say two things, and slow him down politely.
6. **Three verbs a day:** Each day, say three true sentences, one from each group: `Читам ...`, `Учим ...`, `Пишем ...`—then the same about Marko: `Марко чита ...`

## Self-check

Translate: “I’m learning Serbian. I read Cyrillic and Latin. Marko reads the newspaper.”

Answer: `Учим српски. Читам ћирилицу и латиницу. Марко чита новине.` · `Učim srpski. Čitam ćirilicu i latinicu. Marko čita novine.`

Translate: “Can you help me? What does ‘predmet’ mean? — Of course!”

Answer: `Можеш ли да ми помогнеш? Шта значи „предмет”? — Наравно!` · `Možeš li da mi pomogneš? Šta znači „predmet”? — Naravno!`
