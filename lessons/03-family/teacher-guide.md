# Lesson 3 teacher guide — My family

## Target

The learner can identify and introduce at least three real people using `Ovo je moj/moja ...` and state one true family fact with `Imam` or `Nemam`.

## Before the lesson

- Ask the learner to bring a few family photos or use a hand-drawn family map.
- Confirm the words the family actually uses (`mama/majka`, `tata/otac`, partner labels).
- Record the dialogue naturally and slowly in the selected dialect.
- Show Cyrillic and Latin equally; do not require Cyrillic writing yet.

## Suggested sequence (60 minutes)

1. **Cumulative conversation — 6 min:** Full greeting and `Kako si/ste?` exchange, then one natural follow-up. Note weak phrases for later recycling rather than stopping for a drill.
2. **Personal hook — 5 min:** Use a real photo of Marko or another important person. Say `Ovo je moj/moja ...`; let meaning emerge and invite questions.
3. **Dialogue — 8 min:** Listen for relationships, then read in Latin and switch roles. After the letter bridge, return to the same exchange in Cyrillic rather than introducing a separate decoding text.
4. **Personal vocabulary — 7 min:** Select only 6–8 relationship words Amaya actually needs. Use the Cyrillic-led table after each meaning and pronunciation is established; treat the rest as optional reference.
5. **Useful pattern and photo match — 7 min:** Notice `moj/moja` through selected people, then match Cyrillic phrases to real photos and retrieve two from memory. Avoid a full gender or case lecture.
6. **Extended reading, retelling, and discussion — 11 min:** Read the expanded “A photo” for gist, relationships, locations, and one inference. Retell from three prompts, then compare it with a real photo. Allow mixed language while recasting useful Serbian.
7. **Pronunciation — 4 min:** Model `lj/nj` in whole words and names; briefly recycle pure `e/o` or one earlier contrast.
8. **Cyrillic bridge — 6 min:** Introduce `Б Р Н П Д Ц Љ Њ`, decode familiar targets, then reread the dialogue in Cyrillic. Latin remains available for checking after a full attempt.
9. **Family interview and photo conversation — 4 min:** Ask and answer about several real people, locations, and one additional detail. Follow one interesting answer instead of covering every prompt.
10. **Exit performance — 2 min:** Without notes, introduce three people and state one true family fact.

## Important grammar note

The dialogue contains `jednog brata` and `jednu sestru`. Treat these as whole useful chunks. Do not teach the accusative paradigm yet. If the number is not needed, `Imam brata/sestru` is enough.

## Answer key

- Practice A: `moj brat`, `moja mama`, `moj dečko`, `moja sestra`, `moj tata`, `moja devojka`.
- Practice B: `Ko je ovo? — Ovo je moj brat, Luka. Imam jednu sestru. Ovo je moja sestra, Ana.`

## Session note for Lesson 4

Add a dated file in `notes/` recording which family words were genuinely useful, whether `moj/moja` was selected reliably, which Cyrillic targets were decoded, and whether `lj/nj` affected intelligibility.

## Cyrillic boundary

English leads instructions and Latin leads all new language in Lesson 3. The learner is responsible only for the explicit target set and five familiar decoding words. The full Cyrillic reading is supported exposure after comprehension, not evidence that she should already read Cyrillic independently.
